# New moon and full moon journaling with your chart

Moon journaling gets personal when you look at where each new moon or full moon lands in your own birth chart. The sign of the lunation and the house it falls in for you point to an area of life to write about. At the new moon, people often write about what is starting and what a small first step could be. At the full moon, they write about what has come into view and what they're ready to set down.

Your own words stay at the center. We think of the moon as a rhythm to reflect with. It doesn't promise outcomes.

## Why moon journaling often feels generic

Most moon prompts are written for everyone at once, and it shows.

**The same prompts for everyone, every month.** A list of fifty questions can be a nice place to start. After a few cycles, the questions stop sounding like your life.

**Manifestation framing.** A lot of moon content turns writing into a performance, where the page is a place to order results. If that framing has never sat right with you, you're not alone.

**Sun-sign versions.** Some prompts get sorted by sun sign. That's one placement out of ten, and it leaves the rest of your chart out of the conversation.

There's nothing wrong with any of these. They just stop short of the part that makes the practice yours.

## Making it personal: where the lunation lands in your chart

**The sign, and the house it falls in for you.** Every new moon and full moon happens in a sign. Your birth chart divides the sky into twelve houses, and in astrological tradition each house is linked with an area of life, like home, work, relationships or rest. Finding the house a lunation falls in for you gives your writing a place to start. Houses come from your birth time, so the more exact the time, the more this part lines up.

**Placements it sits close to.** Sometimes a new or full moon lands near one of your own planets. Some people like to notice that and write a line about it. It's context, and it doesn't call for alarm.

**Your words come first.** The sky gives a frame. What goes on the page is yours. If a prompt doesn't fit the month you're having, write about the month you're having.

## Questions for one lunation

You can do this with a notebook and ten minutes. Look up the sign and house of the next lunation if you like, or skip that and start writing.

**New moon: beginning gently.**
- What is quietly forming in the part of life this moon touches for you?
- What would a small first step look like?
- What can wait until later?

**Full moon: looking at what's visible.**
- What came into view since the new moon?
- What felt heavier than you expected?
- What are you ready to set down?

**Between the two.** A one-line check-in you can repeat on any day: *What am I noticing right now?*

Keep the pages together. A few cycles in, they start to read like a record of your own rhythm.

## Questions people ask

**What do people journal about on a new moon?**
Often what's beginning. Questions about what's forming, what a small first step could be, and what can wait are a gentle place to start.

**What are good full moon journal prompts?**
Ones that look at what's become visible: what came into view since the new moon, what felt heavy, and what you're ready to set down.

**How do I find which house the new moon is in for me?**
Look up the degree and sign of the new moon, then find where that degree falls in your birth chart. Most chart tools show this once you've entered your birth date, time and place.

**Do I need my birth time for moon journaling?**
It helps for houses, since they come from your birth time. Without it you can still work with the sign of the lunation and any placements it sits close to.

**Do I have to set intentions every new moon?**
No. Some people do and some don't. A new moon can be a time to notice, or a time to rest.

**Is moon journaling the same as manifesting?**
It doesn't have to be. Here it's a way to reflect on a rhythm, with no promise attached to what you write.
