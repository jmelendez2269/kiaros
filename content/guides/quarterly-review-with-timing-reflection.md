# How to do a quarterly review with astrology

A quarterly review with timing starts with your own account of the last 90 days: what helped, what was hard, and what kept coming back. Then you set that beside the sky that moved in relation to your birth chart, like major transits, retrogrades and the new and full moons. The aim is recognition. You notice patterns worth a second look, name where rest belongs next quarter, and carry one small experiment forward.

We treat the timing as a pattern to reflect with. It isn't a verdict on the quarter, and it doesn't predict the next one.

## Why most quarterly reviews feel like scorecards

**Goal apps that only ask what you shipped.** They're good at counting. They rarely ask how the quarter felt or what it took out of you.

**Season roundups that never meet your words.** Astrology recaps can describe the sky for a whole season. They can't say what that season was like for you.

**A quiet quarter can still be a good one.** If the last three months were slow, that might have been rest, or recovery, or something forming under the surface. A review is a place to notice that without marking it down.

## What timing adds to a quarterly reflection

**Your own account first.** Before looking at any chart, write down what helped, what was hard and what shifted. This keeps your experience at the center, so the sky doesn't write the story for you.

**Then the sky that moved.** Look at the major transits to your placements over those 90 days, any retrogrades, and the new and full moons. The positions are measurable. What they mean comes from astrological tradition, and that's worth keeping in view.

**Observations you can keep or set aside.** Sometimes a stretch of the sky lines up with something you lived through. Sometimes it doesn't. Either way, you decide what counts.

## Closing a quarter: questions to sit with

Give yourself half an hour and a page. Answer the ones that pull at you.

**What gathered momentum, and what kept coming back?** A theme, a person, a worry, a project.

**Where did rest happen, and where did it get skipped?** What happened in the weeks after?

**Which weeks felt most like you?** If you're curious, look at what the sky was doing then.

**What would you like more room for next quarter?** In your own words.

**One small experiment to carry forward.** Something to try, with no success metric attached.

## Questions people ask

**What questions are good for a quarterly review?**
Questions about what gathered momentum, what kept coming back, where rest happened or got skipped, and what you'd like more room for next.

**How do I do a quarterly review with astrology?**
Write your own account of the quarter first. Then look at the major transits to your chart, any retrogrades and the lunations across those 90 days, and notice where they line up with what you lived.

**What transits are worth looking at for the last quarter?**
Many people start with the slower planets, since their transits last long enough to shape a season. The new and full moons add a monthly layer.

**How is a reflective quarterly review different from an OKR review?**
An OKR review measures results against targets. A reflective review looks at what the quarter was like and what you want to carry forward. Some people do both.

**What if my quarter felt quiet or unproductive?**
That's worth noticing without calling it a failure. Quiet stretches often turn out to be rest or groundwork.

**How often do people do a timing reflection?**
Quarterly is a common rhythm. Some people add a short monthly check-in, and some only look back once a year. Whatever pace you'll keep is a fine pace.
