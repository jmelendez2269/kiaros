# A clearer way to look back: Reflections, memory, and Yearly Unwrapped

Most days are too close to see clearly. A good conversation can disappear into a busy week. A difficult season can make it hard to remember what was also working. Looking back is useful, but only when it helps you recognize your own life rather than turning it into a scorecard.

Kairos now gives you a gentler way to do that. New monthly and quarterly reflections bring together the information you have chosen to record, show the moments and patterns that stand out, and offer a small amount of direction for what comes next. At the end of the year, Yearly Unwrapped gathers those threads into a wider view.

## Why we built this

Reflection should be grounded in what actually happened. That means a report needs to show its work: which journal entries, notes, intentions, or other records shaped an observation. It also needs to leave room for uncertainty. A repeated phrase is a clue, not a diagnosis. A quiet month is not a failed month.

The new reflection system is designed around a few simple principles:

- Your recorded experience comes first.
- Every insight can be traced back to the information that supports it.
- Goals are included as a light thread, so they can add context without taking over the reflection.
- You can confirm, correct, or dismiss an observation.
- Sparse information is shown as sparse information. Kairos does not invent a complete story from an empty period.

## Monthly reflections

At the close of each month, Kairos can create a reflection from your eligible journal entries, daily notes, captures, completed tasks, intentions, and other records you have allowed it to use.

The report can include:

- an opening that names the shape of the month;
- a few meaningful moments;
- observations about themes or recurring experiences;
- a short look ahead with one focus and a few gentle experiments;
- links to the source records behind each passage.

The purpose is not to tell you what the month meant. It is to make it easier to see what you already lived through.

## Quarterly reflections

Quarterly reflections widen the lens. Instead of treating three months as three isolated reports, Kairos looks for movement across the period: what gathered momentum, what changed, what kept returning, and what may deserve more space next quarter.

Your authored quarterly review still matters. Wins, challenges, pivots, and intentions you wrote yourself remain distinct from Kairos-generated observations, so your own interpretation does not get blurred into an automated summary.

## A small, intentional role for goals

Goals appear only when they help connect the reflection to something you said mattered. Kairos keeps this section short and separate from the main narrative. It may point out how an intention showed up in the month or suggest one small way to carry it forward.

This balance matters. A reflection should include your goals without making every experience answer to productivity. Rest, relationships, uncertainty, joy, and change deserve room even when they do not map neatly to a goal.

## Better memory in conversations

Kairos can also use relevant, consented journal memories when you are talking with Stelloquy. Instead of retrieving the newest entries simply because they are new, the system looks for records related to your question and shows the supporting sources.

You remain in control of this. Journal participation and conversational memory use are separate choices. Only records with the appropriate consent are eligible, and the response can point you back to the original journal entry.

## Yearly Unwrapped

Yearly Unwrapped is the broadest view: a personal record of the year as it actually unfolded.

It includes:

- chapters that show how your recorded days were distributed across the year;
- turning points, discoveries, and rhythms when the evidence supports them;
- a letter to carry into the next year;
- links back to monthly and quarterly reflections;
- an optional private sharing card.

The sharing card is deliberately user-controlled. Nothing is published automatically. You choose the words, review them, and download a PNG card containing only that text.

## How to use the new features

1. Open **Reflections** from the Year section.
2. Choose **Monthly**, **Quarterly**, or **Yearly Unwrapped**.
3. Select the period you want to review and start the reflection.
4. Open **Why am I seeing this?** beneath a passage to review its supporting records.
5. Mark an observation as accurate, correct it, or dismiss it. Your feedback helps future reflections respect what you know about yourself.
6. If Kairos offers an intention, carry it forward only if it feels useful. Add an outcome later to record what you noticed; no success metric is required.
7. In Yearly Unwrapped, choose **Choose a moment to share** if you want a private card. Enter your own words and download the PNG when it feels ready.

You can also enable automatic period-end reflections in the reflection preferences. Confirm your timezone first so month, quarter, and year boundaries match your local life. Automatic generation is opt-in.

## A reflection is an invitation

The best outcome is not a more impressive report. It is a more honest relationship with your own memory: remembering what helped, noticing what changed, and carrying forward what still feels alive.

Kairos is here to help you make that space.
